BOMs, spend cubes, and warranty claims

A hands-on primer. Each section explains one kind of data, then lets you click through the real sample dataset from our repo (data/sample/). The company in it, Prairie Utility Vehicles, is fictional.

The big picture

Three teams, three records of the same parts

ENGINEERING

Bill of materials

What goes into each product. Keyed by the company's internal part number.

PROCUREMENT

Spend cube

Every purchase: what was bought, from whom, for how much. Keyed by the supplier's part number and a text description.

SERVICE

Warranty claims

What broke in the field and what the fix cost. Keyed by failure code and free text. The part number is often missing.

The track asks you to connect the first two. Afterlife connects all three. Each record describes the same physical parts, but with different IDs, different words, and different units.

1 · Bill of materials (BOM)

A recipe for a product

A BOM lists every part needed to build one unit, and how many. Real BOMs are trees: a vehicle contains assemblies, which contain sub-assemblies, which contain parts. Each row says "parent contains qty_per of child".

What a real multi-level BOM looks like

L0 UV-400 Utility vehicle
L1 ├─ 9100-DRV Drivetrain assy ×1
L2 │ ├─ 9110-AXL Rear axle assy ×1
L3 │ │ └─ 100-4471-02 BRG,BALL,6204 ×4
L2 │ └─ 400-2205-03 BELT,CVT ×1
L1 └─ 9200-HYD Hydraulic assy ×1
L2    └─ 200-1180-01 SEAL KIT ×2

To get the total quantity of a part per vehicle, multiply down the tree. This is called "exploding" the BOM. Our sample keeps it flat (single level) for the MVP.

Things to know

  • EBOM / MBOM / SBOM. Engineering, manufacturing, and service BOMs are different views of the same product. The service BOM lists what can be sold as a spare, which matters a lot for us.
  • Where-used. The reverse question: which products contain this part? Shared parts are where the leverage is.
  • Descriptions are ERP shorthand, for example BRG,BALL,6204-2RS: noun first, then attributes, comma-separated.
  • A BOM has no prices. Cost comes from purchasing, which is exactly why the two need linking.

2 · Spend cube

Every dollar the company spends, sliced on a few axes

A spend cube starts as a flat list of purchase lines from purchase orders and invoices. Each line is tagged on a few dimensions, and that's what makes it a "cube": you can slice total spend by any of them. Consultants usually describe the three main axes as what (category), who from (supplier), and who bought it (plant or business unit), with time as a fourth.

Things to know

  • Lines use the supplier's part number and wording, not the BOM's.
  • Units vary. A price can be per each, or per box of 10 (BX10). You have to convert before comparing.
  • Supplier names are messy ("Acme Inc", "ACME Corp."), so building a cube means cleaning and classifying them.
  • Spare and warranty parts are often bought by a different team (service) from a distributor, at a much higher price.
  • Real cubes are huge: millions of lines, including things not in any BOM (office supplies, freight, services).

3 · Warranty claims

What broke, and what it cost to fix

A dealer fixes a unit under warranty and files a claim to get paid back by the manufacturer. Claims record the unit, when it was built, when it failed, a failure code, a text note, and the labor and parts cost. The causal part field, meaning which part actually failed, is often blank or wrong. In our sample it's blank about 58% of the time.

4 · Putting it together

One bearing, three records

Here is the same physical part as each team sees it. The matching job is to figure out that these rows are all one thing, without an answer key.

The questions linking unlocks
  • What did we actually pay per unit for each part, after converting units?
  • How much more does service pay for the same part as a spare?
  • Which BOMs share a part, so a supplier problem hits all of them at once?
  • Which supplier's part is driving warranty cost, and could we recover it?
Glossary

Words you'll hear from Kearney judges

BOM
Bill of materials: the parts and quantities that make up a product.
Spend cube
All purchase spend, classified by category, supplier, and buying unit (plus time) so you can slice it.
Part number (PN)
The internal ID. Different from the supplier PN and the manufacturer PN (MPN).
UoM
Unit of measure (EA, BX10, KG…). Prices are per UoM.
Where-used
Every product or assembly that contains a given part.
Price harmonization
Paying the best price you already get for a part everywhere you buy it.
Aftermarket
Spare parts and service after the sale. Often high-margin for the OEM, and badly controlled on the buying side.
Supplier recovery
Charging a supplier back for warranty costs caused by its defective part.
Claim rate
Claims per 1,000 units built, per build month. Normalizes for how many units were made.
Should-cost
What a part ought to cost based on materials and process, used to challenge supplier prices.
ERP / SAP
The software holding all of this. BOMs live in SAP tables STKO/STPO, purchase orders in EKKO/EKPO, and the part master in MARA.
