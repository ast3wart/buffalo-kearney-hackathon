Why

Plants run on parts no one can see are about to fail

Hidden discontinued parts

Machines run on controllers, drives and screens the manufacturer stopped making. Most plants have no full list of what is inside their cabinets.

Maintenance plans miss them

Scheduled maintenance covers what wears: bearings, belts, oil. An electronic part that fails without warning is not on the schedule. Only 55% of plants have a plan for obsolete components.

One failure, weeks of outage

New PLCs take 10–24 weeks; a discontinued one exists only on the resale market. 44% of plants have an equipment interruption every month.

Expansion · condition sensing hypothesis until the research lands

The registry says where to look. A thermal sensor says how it is doing.

Why heat

  • Failing drives, power supplies and loose connections tend to run hot before they stop ⚠ cite
  • Thermal checks of electrical cabinets are already normal maintenance practice ⚠ cite
  • Today our health status comes from age and lifecycle. Heat adds a measurement.

Why us

  • The registry already ranks the cabinets that matter: line stopper, discontinued, no spare at any plant
  • A plant needs a handful of sensors, not hundreds
  • No hardware of our own at first: an off-the-shelf thermal camera on the same site walk as the photos
