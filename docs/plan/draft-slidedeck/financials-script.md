# Financials script: slides 6–7 (Brandon, right after Asher's demo)

About 90 seconds. Numbers are Toledo in the demo portfolio. Formulas are in appendix A1.

## Transition (Asher's demo ends on the Fort Wayne spare)
- "Asher just showed you **what** we find. I'll show what it's **worth**, and how we get paid."
- "The two spares at Fort Wayne are exactly where the money comes from."

## Slide 6: What we count, and what we only show
- **Two kinds of value, and we keep them apart.**
- **Left: direct savings, $47.7k a year at Toledo.**
  - Lower spares carrying cost, planned buys instead of emergency buys, a sister plant's spare instead of a purchase, fewer rush premiums.
  - Every line traces back to parts in the registry. The customer can audit it.
  - Plus $79k of idle spares cash, freed once.
- **Right: downtime avoided, about $19k a year. An estimate.**
  - Formula: *chance it fails × hours the spare saves × lost margin per hour × share of output never made up.*
  - Conservative: only the spare rescues we actually found, one failure per spare, over 3 years, discounted at 10%. Outage capped at 10–25 days.
  - It depends on judgment calls, so **we show it, but never price it.**
- **The line to land:** "The business case stands on the left side alone. Downtime is upside."

## Slide 7: Business model
- "So how do we get paid? **We charge for what the customer can count, not for what we say they saved.**"
- **Three steps, and each one grows the account:**
  1. **Onboard a plant:** one-time scan, $1–2k.
  2. **Registry subscription:** $4–10k a year per plant, tiered by the number of critical parts.
  3. **Sensors:** a monthly fee per monitored point, hardware included.
- **Why not a share of savings?** "Then we'd be grading our own fee. Our fee stays fixed. The savings are the customer's business case."
- **Payback:** "Toledo pays $9.5k a year and saves $47.7k. That's paid back in under 3 months."
- **Growth:** more plants per company → more sensors per plant → more companies per fund.
- **Hand off to the close:** "That's why our next step is 3 paid plant pilots and 1 portfolio."

## If a judge asks
- **"Isn't downtime a guess?"** "Yes, so it's a range and it's never in the price. Pilots replace our assumptions with the plant's own outage logs."
- **"Where do the tier prices come from?"** "Our money model on the demo plants. Pilots test willingness to pay."
- **"Margins?"** "It costs us $0.3–1.8k a year to serve a plant, against a $4–10k fee."
