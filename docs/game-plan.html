The real problem

It's a matching problem dressed up as a finance problem

Engineering owns the BOM and procurement owns the spend cube. They describe the same physical part in different ways, so a VLOOKUP gets maybe 30% of the way. Here is what the "same part" looks like in real data:

SourcePart IDDescriptionUoMPrice
BOM · Pump P-200100-4471-02BRG,BALL,6204-2RS,20MMEAnone
Spend · SKF Dist.6204-2RSHDeep groove ball bearing 20x47x14 sealedEA$4.12
Spend · Grainger1ZGU7BEARING 6204 2RS C3BX/10$58.90
Spend · AftermarketSP-100-4471SPARE BRG KIT P-200 (WARRANTY)KIT$31.00

Those four rows are one bearing bought at roughly $4.12, $5.89, and ~$15 per unit (after unit and kit conversion). The BOM tells you it goes into the P-200 and, as it turns out, 11 other products. Once that link exists, three things open up:

True cost per BOM

Roll up what you actually paid for every component, weighted by volume, including spare and warranty spend that today is invisible to product P&L.

Price variance

The same part from different suppliers or plants at different prices. "Pay everyone the best price" is a dollar figure you can put on a slide.

Shared-part leverage

A part used in 12 BOMs and bought from 3 suppliers can be bundled into one contract. This also hands you Track 1 (platforming) for free in the pitch.

Shock simulation

"Tariff on country X goes up 25%. Which products get hit, and by how much?" You can only answer that with the link in place, and it's timely.

What makes you different

Five moves most teams won't make

  1. Ground truth you can measure. Write a synthetic data generator that plants known BOM↔spend links and then corrupts them on purpose: abbreviations, supplier part numbers, box-of-10 units, typos, currency, missing crosswalks. Then report precision and recall on the slide ("we recover 93% of links at 98% precision"). Judges almost never see a hackathon team measure its own accuracy.
  2. Confidence tiers plus a review queue. Auto-accept the high-confidence matches, send the gray zone to a human with one-click accept or reject, and drop the rest. That's how a real Kearney engagement would use the tool, and it answers "what if the AI is wrong?" before anyone asks.
  3. Lead with "spend coverage". The headline metric is "% of spend tied to a BOM" (for example, going from 31% to 88%). Consultants think in coverage. Put it in the top-left corner of the app.
  4. Talk in Kearney lever language. Label your savings as price harmonization, volume bundling, supplier consolidation, spec standardization, and aftermarket pricing. Show a $ range for each, not just one number.
  5. Pitch it as a week-1 diagnostic. "In the first two weeks of a cost engagement, analysts stitch this together in Excel. We do it in 10 minutes." That puts the value in their own workflow and their own billable time.

Architecture

The matching pipeline

Use cheap and certain checks first and save the expensive or fuzzy ones for the leftovers. Every link keeps the reason it matched, so you can show your work.

1 · NormalizeUppercase, expand abbreviations (BRG→BEARING), parse units (BX/10→10 EA), convert currency
2 · Exact keysInternal PN, crosswalk table, manufacturer PN (MPN) after stripping suffixes
3 · AttributesExtract size, thread, material, and spec codes such as 6204 or M8x1.25 and compare them
4 · Fuzzy + embedrapidfuzz token score plus embedding similarity, blocked by category
5 · LLM judgeOnly the gray zone. Returns match / no-match plus a reason
6 · ReviewHuman queue, and decisions feed back into the crosswalk

Data model (keep it this small)

bom(product, parent, part_no, qty_per, uom, level)
spend(po_line, supplier, supplier_pn, desc, qty,
      uom, unit_price, ccy, date, plant, country,
      spend_type: production|spare|warranty)
link(part_no, po_line, method, confidence, status)

Stack for 35 hours

  • Python: pandas, rapidfuzz, an embedding model, and an LLM API for the judge step
  • Streamlit for the UI. It's fast, and Plotly works inside it
  • One standout visual: a Sankey from supplier → part → product
  • SQLite or plain parquet. No database server

Demo

A 4-minute demo script

  1. Hook (30s). "This company spends $180M a year. Today they can tie 31% of it to a product. So what does the P-200 pump really cost?"
  2. Run the link (45s). Hit one button. Coverage climbs to 88%, and the confidence tiers fill in.
  3. The bearing (45s). Click one part. It's bought from 3 suppliers at 3 prices, used in 12 products, and has a spare kit at a 4× markup.
  4. Savings (60s). Show the lever table with $ ranges and the Sankey.
  5. Shock (30s). Move a tariff slider and watch which products turn red.
  6. Trust (30s). "93% recall at 98% precision on planted data, and humans review the gray zone." Then close.
