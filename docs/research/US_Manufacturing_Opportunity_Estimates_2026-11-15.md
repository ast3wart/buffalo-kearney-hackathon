# US Manufacturing Opportunity Estimates

*November 14, 2026*

## US manufacturing: annual planning and diligence opportunities

I’d count **companies for (a) and (b)**, but **transactions or sale processes for (c) and (d)**. Using US manufacturers with operating plants as the default scope, these are planning estimates—not four additive market segments.

| Opportunity | Estimated number in a typical year | Evidence and calculation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **(a) Multi-plant manufacturers doing equipment CapEx or obsolescence planning** | **~8,000–18,000 companies**; midpoint **~12,000** | **Estimate.** The 2022 Census-based count is 239,265 manufacturing firms, and 2022 business-pattern data show roughly 285,500 manufacturing establishments. Those figures establish scale, **not** a count of firms owning multiple manufacturing plants. I assume ~10,000–20,000 qualifying multi-plant firms and that ~75–90% undertake some equipment planning annually. The planning rate is an assumption, not a survey finding. [1](https://nam.org/mfgdata/facts-about-manufacturing-expanded/) · [2](https://data.census.gov/table/CBP2022.CB2200CBP?codeset=naics%7E31-33&d=ECNSVY+Business+Patterns+County+Business+Patterns&g=010XX00US) |
| **(b) Manufacturing PE portfolio companies in their hold period** | **~2,000–4,000 companies in a given year**; midpoint **~3,000** | **Estimate of a stock, not annual new deals.** An industry study puts _all-sector_ US PE-backed companies at approximately 21,000 in 2024 and reports 1.9 million PE-backed US manufacturing workers; neither figure directly counts manufacturing portfolio companies. A separate portfolio database counts active holdings but groups many manufacturers with nonmanufacturing “Industrials,” so it cannot supply a clean manufacturing total. [3](https://www.investmentcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/EY-AIC-2024-Economic-contribution-of-the-US-private-equity-sector.pdf) · [4](https://www.withintelligence.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/pe-harvest-report.pdf) |
| **(c) Acquisitions of manufacturing companies involving equipment diligence** | **~650–1,150 acquisitions/year**; midpoint **~900** | **Estimate applied to a measured deal-series count.** Travelers, citing PitchBook, reports **1,667 US manufacturing M&A transactions in 2024**. I assume ~40–70% warrant substantive diligence on production equipment, condition, replacement needs or related CapEx. Deal counts do **not** establish that such diligence actually occurred. [5](https://www.travelers.com/resources/business-industries/manufacturing/manufacturing-mergers-acquisitions) |
| **(d) Sell-side/vendor diligence before a manufacturing-company exit** | **~200–500 sale processes/year**; midpoint **~300** | **Estimate, not a measured US manufacturing count.** Applying a cautious ~12–30% incidence of formal seller-commissioned diligence to the 1,667-deal benchmark gives roughly 200–500. An EY **survey** of 100 PE professionals found vendor-diligence material in 72% of sampled exits that met/exceeded targets and 69% of those that did not. That is evidence it is common in this selected PE sample—not a rate applicable to _all_ manufacturing sales. If “vendor diligence” means **equipment-specific** sell-side work rather than any formal vendor diligence, use a narrower **~50–200/year** working estimate. [5](https://www.travelers.com/resources/business-industries/manufacturing/manufacturing-mergers-acquisitions) · [6](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-gl/noindex/documents/ey-gl-pe-exit-readiness-study-2025-full-findings-report-10-25.pdf) |

**Pushback on overlap:** Do **not** add these midpoints to claim ~16,000 distinct customers. **(b) is substantially inside (a)** wherever a PE-owned manufacturer has multiple plants. **(c) and (d) can describe opposite sides of the same sale**, while the target may also have appeared in (a) and (b) that year. The cleanest framing is **~8,000–18,000 recurring multi-plant planning accounts**, plus **~650–1,150 acquisition events** and **~200–500 seller-side processes** that may create a different buying moment—not necessarily a new account. Also, (b) is a _portfolio stock present during the year_; multiplying it by an annual deal count would confuse holdings with transactions.
